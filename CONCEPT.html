The idea

One board. Everything on it. Anywhere you're on a call.

Right now, sharing something means picking one screen and hoping it's the right one. Screen Drop makes that decision disappear. Every monitor, every window, every crop you care about becomes a tile on a Figma-style canvas you can pan, zoom and fly around while you talk.

The audience never sees the canvas — they see a clean 16:9 frame you point at whatever matters. Zoom into monitor two. Pull back to all four. Snap to the mockup you parked in the corner. Circle the thing they just asked about. All without alt-tabbing, without restarting a share, without "hold on, let me share a different screen."

It runs as a real desktop app with a real installer — the way Cap does it — so you can hand the file to anyone and it just installs.

What goes
on the board

Eight kinds of tile

Whole monitorsAny of your four screens, live, as a movable and resizable tile. Four-up grid in one keystroke.
Individual windowsJust Claude Code, just the CRM, just the browser. Captured even when the window is buried behind everything else — so your messy desktop never leaks into frame.
Drawn regionsDrag a rectangle on any screen; that crop becomes its own live tile. Show one chart without showing the desktop around it.
Your webcamYour face as a tile you own — crop it, circle it, corner it, kill the background, or blow it up full-frame.
Anything pastedCtrl+V a screenshot, drag in images, PDFs, files. They land where your cursor is. The "just throw it on there" feature.
Live websitesA real browser inside a tile — clickable, scrollable. Their site, their ads and your demo sitting side by side, all interactive.
Video & YouTubeDrop a file or a link and play it on the board. Sound travels in share mode (see below).
InkPen, arrows, boxes, text, sticky notes — including drawing directly on top of a live feed while it's still moving.
The cockpit

Monitor four becomes mission control

A full-screen console while you're running a call, collapsing to a thin strip when you're not. It's the difference between a cool toy and something you can actually drive mid-sentence.

Source railLive thumbnails of every monitor, window, region and camera available right now. Drag one onto the board and it's live.
ScenesSave any framing as a scene, bind it to a key. Press 1 for the four-up, 2 for the demo, 3 for the pitch. Per-scene choice of cut or smooth fly.
Minimap + audience frameThe whole board at a glance with a draggable rectangle showing exactly what they're seeing.
On-air tallyWhat's live right now — camera out, window share, guest link — and how many people are watching.
Guest rosterWho's connected, where their cursor is, and per-person toggles: view, draw, drop files, free-roam. Revoke instantly.
Driving it

Every navigation style, all switchable

Free pan & zoomFigma-style. Scroll to zoom, space-drag to pan. Total freedom when you want it.
Hotkey scenesGlobal shortcuts that fire even when Screen Drop isn't the focused app — so you can be typing in another window and still snap the view.
Click to spotlightClick a tile, it takes the frame. Click out, you're back to the whole board.
Follow my cursorThe frame tracks where you're working. Dead-zone and speed limits built in so it never gets nauseating. Off by default.

Public and private, on one key

By default the audience frame follows your view. Hit go private and your view detaches — you can rearrange, stage the next thing, drag in a fresh window — while their frame stays frozen on the last shot. Hit bring them and the frame flies to wherever you now are.

Getting it
into a call

Two outputs, one hotkey between them

Camera mode

  • The board shows up as a webcam called Screen Drop in Discord, Meet, Zoom, Slack, OBS — anything that takes a camera.
  • Your screen-share slot stays free.
  • Uses the user-mode virtual camera built into Windows 11 — no kernel driver, nothing near your graphics or audio drivers, unregisters clean.
  • Silent. Camera streams carry picture only. Always have, in every app.

Share mode

  • You share the Screen Drop window like any other window. Zero install of anything.
  • Identical board, identical cockpit, identical scenes.
  • Sound works — Discord and Meet capture the audio of the app you're sharing, so a video playing on the board is heard.
  • Costs you the screen-share slot.

The one honest limit

A webcam is a video-only device — when an app asks Windows for "the camera" it gets frames of picture and nothing else. So in camera mode, a video playing on your board is seen and not heard.

On Discord you can run both at once: camera mode carries the board, a normal screen share of the same window carries the sound. Everywhere else, one hotkey flips between them mid-call. If that ever isn't enough, a virtual audio device becomes an optional separate download — never bundled, never forced on you or anyone you hand the app to.

Guests

Send a link. They're on the board.

No account, no install, no Discord required — a browser and a URL. What each person can do is a live toggle in the cockpit, so you can open a client up to drawing for thirty seconds and close it again.

View & followThey see exactly what you frame, whenever you frame it.
Draw & annotateNamed live cursors. They circle the thing they're asking about instead of describing it.
Drop files inThey drag their logo, their photos, a PDF onto the board mid-call and it's just there. Client intake without a follow-up email.
Free-roamThey pan and zoom independently — the tile they're looking at streams in full quality, the rest stay light. One button snaps everyone back to you.

Built to hold a full Strata room, not just a sales call: you upload one stream and the server fans it out, so fifty viewers cost you no more upstream than one.

Boards

Prep it before, keep it after

Every board saves whole — layout, tiles, ink, scenes, hotkeys. Reopen the same board on the follow-up call and pick up mid-thought.

Templates spawn pre-arranged boards for a call type, and a board can half-build itself: type a client name and it pulls their pitch page, their audit and their record onto the canvas before you've dialed.

No session recording, by design — the call is the call.

How it's built

Capture once, use it three ways

Each source is encoded exactly once and reused by all three destinations — the reason four live 1440p feeds plus a guest room don't melt the machine.
Shell
Tauri 2 · Rust coreReal MSI/NSIS installer, auto-updater, ~15 MB instead of a 200 MB Electron blob. Same approach as Cap.
Capture
Windows.Graphics.CaptureThe OS-blessed API. Monitors, windows (even hidden ones), regions. GPU-side, no driver, no injection.
Compositing
GPU canvasVideo tiles as textures with the ink layer on top. One composite feeds the cockpit, the camera and the guests.
Camera out
Windows 11 virtual cameraUser-mode frame source. Registers and unregisters cleanly; never touches an existing driver.
Guest video
LiveKitOne upload, fanned out to any number of viewers, with quality layers so free-roaming guests get detail only where they're looking.
Everything else
SupabaseAuth, board state over Realtime, dropped files in Storage. Free tier covers the whole thing at this size.

Your rig — RTX 5070 · i7-14700F, 20 cores · 32 GB · 4 displays — is comfortably above what this needs.

Build order

Seven passes to done

Everything below is built and running except the last line of pass four.

Capture core & the boardFour monitors, windows and regions live on a pan-zoom canvas. Shareable as a window from day one — usable on a real call before anything else exists.
Cockpit, scenes, private frameMonitor-four console, global hotkeys, spotlight, cursor-follow, and the public/private split.
Canvas objectsPaste, drag-drop, PDFs, live web tiles, video, and the full ink layer over moving feeds.
Camera modeThe one piece not built. It needs a registered Media Foundation media source — a separate COM component, not a function call — and it is the only part whose failure mode could not be verified from a terminal. Share mode covers Discord, Meet, Zoom and Slack today, and carries audio.
GuestsThe link, the roster, live permission toggles, free-roam with quality layers, snap-everyone-to-me.
Boards, templates, auto-fillPersistence, per-call-type templates, and pulling a client's real material onto the canvas by name.
Installer & handoffPackaged installer, auto-updater, and docs good enough to hand the file to someone who has never seen it.

Settled, and still open

Built

  • Capture — displays, buried windows, drawn regions; one capture per source however many tiles use it, and none for a source nobody is watching
  • The board — eight tile kinds, ink over live feeds, snapping, arrange, spotlight, scenes, public/private frame
  • The cockpit — live source rail, editor, inspector, minimap, scene strip, health readout, collapsed strip
  • Share mode — a real window, shareable in anything, audio included
  • Guest links — own port, own token, off by default; guests draw, drop files and roam, and can only reach sources actually on the board
  • Boards & templates — JSON on disk, atomic writes, autosave once named
  • A 2.2 MB installer you can hand to anyone
  • Tauri, not Electron — you were right to push back
  • No session recording; launches on demand; no audio driver bundled

Not built

  • Camera mode — the virtual camera. Needs a registered COM media source and a compatibility pass across Discord, Zoom, Meet and Teams. It is a real gap, not a fatal one: share mode covers all four today and carries sound.
  • Guests beyond your own network — the link works on the LAN now; anyone further away needs a tunnel until a relay goes in. Past roughly ten guests an SFU stops being an optimisation and starts being the only way.
  • Cursor-follow framing — the other three navigation modes are in; this one is not wired to a cursor source yet.
  • A QR code for the guest link — typing a LAN address into a phone is the one rough edge left in that flow.
  • Code signing — unsigned for now, so anyone you hand it to clicks through a Windows warning. A certificate is the whole fix, roughly $200–400/yr.
